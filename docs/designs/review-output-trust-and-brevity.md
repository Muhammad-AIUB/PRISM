## Engineering Review Outcome (2026-09-14)

then an outside voice overturned four of those nine. Read this section before the

**The sequencing changed. The measurement harness in "The Assignment" now comes
FIRST, before any implementation.**

The argument that settled it: the success criterion "100% of displayed line numbers

test. The harness is the only artifact that can falsify premise 2, it costs about an

largest error in the reduced plan.

**The three lint rules are gone** (`console.log`, `any`, missing React `key`), and the
base prompt now says outright not to report anything a linter catches. Deleting a rule
removes the instruction and not the habit, which is why the negative instruction matters
more than the deletion did.

**The fixtures stopped being a PHP parity record.** They are now a snapshot of our own
output and a guard against unintended drift. That is a one-way door, taken on purpose,
in the commit that rewrote the prompt. `scripts/regen-fixtures.ts` carries the warning.

**`blocking` is not a rename of `critical`.** A model will call anything critical. The
verdict blocks only on a category a reader can confirm against the diff in seconds:
`auth_weakened`, `contract_changed`, `no_timeout`. An invented category normalises to
`other` and cannot reach the blocking set. That is the guard that stops the verdict
being the score with a new label.

**The score can no longer contradict the verdict.** The model scores the list it
reported, before validation drops anything, so a review could render "Score 88/100"
directly above the word BLOCKING. `reconcileScore()` pulls the number into the band the
prompt itself defines — a blocking review is capped at 60, a nothing-found review is
floored at 80. The score stays in the payload because `mcp-server/index.js` reads it.

**Suggested fixes are checked too.** They were the last surface still displaying a
number nobody had verified: the UI renders "Line {fix.line}" in a badge, and nothing
confirmed it. Fixes cannot use anchors — they quote real code, so they see the raw
selected diff — but `problematic_code` is itself the check. If the quoted line is
really at the claimed line (±3, whitespace-normalised), the claim holds; otherwise the
fix is dropped or shown at file level. That is a stronger check than the first pass
can make, not a weaker one.

**The memory bound is now where it does something.** An earlier decision in this
review put a cap at the fetch boundary; an outside voice showed that frees zero bytes
because `request()` ends with `await response.text()`, which has already allocated the
whole diff. `readBounded()` now consumes the stream and stops at 2MB, cutting on a line
boundary and cancelling the reader so the rest never arrives.

**Still not done, and recorded as such:** a daily Groq rate limit still burns all three
BullMQ attempts and marks a review permanently failed, which is a regression against
the old recoverable-but-ugly behaviour (`TODOS.md` item 1). The merge-commit diff claim
is still unverified — an attempt hit GitHub's unauthenticated rate limit and it needs a
token (item 2). The five-tab layout is still there, with the verdict panel above it
rather than replacing it (item 4).

## Problem Statement

PRism produces a review that a developer with deep experience would not respect, and that
a tired developer cannot read quickly. Two failures, in that order:

1. **It is not checkable.** Nothing verifies that a reported issue points at a file or line
   that actually changed. The model invents line numbers by counting inside `@@` hunk
   headers, and the UI displays them prominently (commit `ca00d28`).
2. **It is not readable.** A 0-100 score plus three tabbed lists plus a fixes tab plus a
   diff tab. The reader has to hunt. The GitHub comment, which is where developers actually
   are, carries only counts.

Quality bar in the user's own words: the output must satisfy a developer with 20, 30 or 50
years of experience, and the reader must not have to "find out with disturb mind the
results."

## Demand Evidence

**None.** Asked directly: "None yet, honestly. No one outside has used it or asked for it."

Recorded as-is because it bounds everything below. Every decision here is reasoned from the
code and from craft, not from observed user behaviour.

## Status Quo

Not established by interview (the user chose to skip the market questions). Known without
asking: a developer reviewing a PR today has their own eyes, a linter that already catches
what PRism's prompt asks for, GitHub's built-in Copilot review, and a funded field of AI
reviewers (CodeRabbit, Greptile, Cursor Bugbot, Qodo). An experienced developer reading a
diff is, today, better at this than `llama-3.3-70b-versatile`. PRism's opening is therefore
some combination of free, quiet, and right.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**Unresolved, by choice.** Three candidates, none selected: solo developers with no second
reviewer (strongest pain, but usually not the veterans the quality bar targets);
price-sensitive developers (a market the author knows from the inside); teams already
drowning in noisy AI comments (best problem, hardest to reach, and the one this design's
"say less" thesis actually serves). Largest open risk in the document.

## Constraints

- `synchronize: false`, `migrationsRun: false`. `schema.sql` is the source of truth; every
  schema change is applied by hand against live production data.
- `test/fixtures/system-prompts.json` is asserted byte-for-byte and cannot be regenerated.
  **26 entries** (13 language combinations × `commit` and `pull_request`). Verified
  composition: 10 carry the JS/TS rules, 8 are base-prompt-only (`none`, `ruby`, `java`,
  `ruby_java` across both targets), the rest carry other language rules.
- `/api/v1` shapes and error envelopes are a wire contract. **Fields may be added, never
  removed.** `mcp-server/index.js` reads `overall_score` in both detail and list responses.
- Render free tier: 512MB, `concurrency: 1`. The per-attempt job budget is derived in
  `review.queue.ts:46-50` as `GROQ_TIMEOUT_MS * (GROQ_MODELS.length + 1) +
  GITHUB_TIMEOUT_MS * 2 + 30_000`. It already assumes every call burns its full ceiling.
- Groq only, two models, fixed order. Model ids, ordering and temperature are measured
  behaviour.
- **Two near-parallel runners** carrying the same bugs in the same places. CLAUDE.md: "a
  change to one usually belongs in the other." Every step names both.

## Premises

All six agreed in session. Premise 3 softened after review.

1. **The model never sees the code.** `pr-review.runner.ts:29` and
   `commit-review.runner.ts:26` both set `DIFF_LIMIT = 8000`, about 200 lines. The fixes
   pass sees 4000 characters (`prompt-builder.service.ts:158`). `detectLanguages()` runs on
   the truncated slice (`pr-review.runner.ts:87-88`, `commit-review.runner.ts:80-81`).
   Nothing tells the user truncation happened.
2. **Unverified line numbers lose experienced developers fastest.** Nothing checks that
   `issue.file` is in the diff or that `issue.line` is in a changed hunk. `toLine()`
   (`fixes.service.ts:36`) only coerces a type. `buildComments()`
   (`pr-review.runner.ts:294`) turns a missing file into the string `'unknown'`.
3. **The value never arrives where the work happens.** `summary-comment.builder.ts` posts a
   score, three counts and a paragraph. `review_comments` rows carry `file_path` and
   `line_number`, which is a *start* on what GitHub's review API needs but not the whole of
   it: that API also requires `commit_id`, `side`, and a line present in the diff at that
   position. Inline commenting is real work, not nearly-free work.
4. **Generic lint rules make experienced developers stop reading.**
   `prompt-builder.service.ts:105-114` flags `console.log`, `any`, missing React `key`.
5. **A 0-100 score is not actionable.** Unstable across re-runs, unverifiable.
6. **Prompt changes must break a fixture on purpose.**

Premise 3 depends on premise 2.

## Approaches Considered

**A: Trust Repair** (chosen, phase 1). Index the diff, feed numbered lines, discard
findings that do not match, replace byte truncation with file selection. M / Low-Med.

**B: Chunked multi-pass** (deferred). Per-file chunks, bounded parallel calls, synthesis.
XL / High. Multiplies calls against Groq free-tier limits inside 512MB, concurrency 1.
Buys capacity no user has asked for. This repo already carries the scar of a job budget
smaller than its work (`a5a0d68`).

**C: Say less, and be right** (chosen, phase 2). Verdict instead of headline score, three
findings worst-first, real code quoted, lint rules replaced. M / Med. Rejected standalone:
presents model-invented numbers confidently and puts that mistake first.

## Recommended Approach

**A then C**, with Phase 1 split so unconditional bug fixes are not gated on measurement.

---

### Phase 1a — unconditional. Two bug fixes, no measurement, no schema change.

**1. `detectLanguages()` on the full diff, not the truncated slice.** One line in each of
two runners (`pr-review.runner.ts:87-88`, `commit-review.runner.ts:80-81`).

**2. File selection instead of byte truncation, in both runners.** Split on `diff --git`,
skip lockfiles, `dist/`, `vendor/`, `*.min.*`, generated and binary files, include whole
files until the budget is spent, never cut mid-hunk. The budget itself stays at 8000 here;
raising it is Phase 1b, because it needs a number nobody has measured yet.

---

### Phase 1b — gated on the baseline measurement (see The Assignment).

**3. Raise the budget to what the headroom actually permits.**
Proposed starting point 40,000 characters (~10-12k tokens) and a 40-file cap, both
provisional. Justify before merge with the harness from The Assignment (not with production
logging, which Render's ephemeral logs make useless):
- Read Groq's `x-ratelimit-remaining-tokens` / `x-ratelimit-reset-tokens` headers **in the
  harness**. `post()` (`ai-client.service.ts:170-225`) does not read response headers today,
  and it should not start doing so just to gather a number.
- Measure **p95 completion latency at the proposed budget against `GROQ_TIMEOUT_MS` (60s)**.
  This, not the job budget, is the real risk: the derived `REVIEW_JOB_TIMEOUT_MS` already
  assumes every call burns 60s, so a larger prompt cannot exceed it. What a larger prompt
  *can* do is push an individual completion past the 60s per-call ceiling, which `post()`
  reports as a soft failure and which cascades to the null-score path. If p95 approaches
  60s, raise `GROQ_TIMEOUT_MS` (the job budget follows automatically) and re-check 512MB.
- Confirm peak buffer with `concurrency: 1`, counting the index from step 5 as well as the
  prompt.
If the headroom is not there, the budget is whatever the headroom allows and the coverage
criterion is restated to match. Do not ship an aspirational number.

**4. Handle 429 as a distinct outcome.**
`callGroqRaw` (`ai-client.service.ts:145-151`) treats every non-ok response identically to
unparseable output: warn, return the HTTP body as `raw`, fall through to the smaller model,
and if that also fails the runner writes a *completed* review whose summary is the literal
rate-limit JSON (`pr-review.runner.ts:105-124`). A 5x larger prompt makes 429 likely rather
than rare, so the graceful-degradation path becomes the normal path and the user-visible
artifact of a trust-repair project is a raw error blob. Required: distinguish 429 from a
parse failure, respect `retry-after` with a bounded in-job wait (the derived budget has
room), and never surface an HTTP error body as `summary`.

**5. New `prism-api/src/diff/hunk-index.ts`, indexing both sides, bounded.**
Per file: `{ added: Map<newLine, text>, removed: Map<oldLine, text>, context: Map<newLine,
text> }`. New-side-only cannot anchor a *deleted* line, and a removed auth check is the
highest-value finding the Phase 2 rules exist to catch.

**Bound it.** Index the selected files plus their context, and cap total indexed lines at
200,000 with explicit degradation past the cap. There is no size guard on diffs anywhere
today (`github-client.service.ts` caps nothing, `diff-cache.service.ts:61` stores the whole
body in Redis); only 8000 characters are ever processed. Three `Map` per file across a
multi-megabyte monorepo diff would be the first unbounded allocation in this pipeline, on a
512MB box.

**6. Feed the model a two-gutter numbered rendering, and have it report a side.**
Both runners send `Review this diff:\n<raw .diff>` today (`pr-review.runner.ts:97`,
`commit-review.runner.ts:90`) and the model counts inside `@@` headers. Replace with:

```
=== src/auth/session.ts ===
 old  new
 116  116 |   const user = await load(req)
 117  117 |   const scope = user.scope
 118    -  | - if (!user.isAdmin) return deny()
   -  118  | + return allow(scope)
```

A removed line consumes no new-side number, so it is addressed by its **old** number with an
explicit side, which is what GitHub's review API does (`side: "LEFT"`). The first-pass JSON
schema gains `side: "added" | "removed"` alongside `line`. Displayed locator for a removal
is `file:oldLine (removed)`.

**7. New `prism-api/src/ai/issue-validator.ts`, after the first AI pass in both runners.**
- Drop any issue whose `file` is not in the index.
- **First-pass issues: exact membership only, no snapping.** Accept `line` only if it is
  itself an added line (when `side: "added"`) or a removed line (when `side: "removed"`).
  Otherwise null the line and render the issue file-level. There is no code field on
  first-pass issues to confirm a near-match against, so a ±N tolerance would degenerate to
  "is there any changed line nearby", which in a dense hunk is almost always true and would
  pass wrong numbers straight through.
- **Second-pass fixes: ±3 with confirmation.** `suggested_fixes` carries
  `problematic_code`, so a near-match is checkable. Normalize by trimming and collapsing
  internal whitespace, accept within ±3 lines, nearest wins, and **store and display the
  snapped line, not the claimed one**. Drop the fix if nothing matches.
- Widen `ReviewIssue.line` to `number | null` in `review.entity.ts` and
  `prism-web/src/lib/types.ts`. Render sites (`parts.tsx:378`, `mcp-server/index.js:59`,
  `pdf-report.service.ts:197`) all use truthy checks and are unaffected.
- Write counts (`kept`, `droppedUnknownFile`, `lineNulled`, `fixesDropped`) into `coverage`.

**8. One nullable `json` column `coverage` on both `reviews` and `commit_reviews`.**
Hand-applied in `schema.sql`. Holds reviewed and skipped file lists plus the step 7 counts.
This is the only reason the success criteria are computable: Render's logs are ephemeral and
not queryable. Surfaced as one sentence: "Reviewed 14 of 31 changed files."
**Blocked on Open Question 5** (owner and rollback for a hand-applied production DDL).

**9. Apply the same treatment to the fixes pass.**
`buildFixesPrompt` still does `diff.slice(0, 4000)` (`prompt-builder.service.ts:158`), which
cuts mid-hunk and, after step 6, would carry the gutter overhead and show *less* real code
than today. Give it the selected, rendered content under its own budget.

### Phase 2 (Approach C): make it readable in five seconds

**10. Replace the language rules with language-agnostic intent rules, in the base block.**
Delete `console.log`, `any`, React `key`. Add, to the **base prompt** rather than to
`getLanguageRules()`: behaviour changed without a test changed; an error path that swallows
its error; an auth or permission check removed or weakened; a new external call with no
timeout; a migration with no rollback; a changed public contract (response shape, error
string). These are not language-specific and belong where every diff sees them, including
the Ruby and Java diffs that get no rules at all today.

Also add an explicit **negative** instruction: "Do not report issues a linter catches:
unused imports, console or print statements, `any`, missing React keys." Deleting the rule
removes the instruction, not the model's disposition, and `llama-3.3-70b-versatile` will
flag `console.log` unprompted.

The first-pass schema also gains `category`, drawn from the rule list above, because step 12
needs a checkable category and the schema is being changed in this commit anyway. `category`
and `side` are stored inside the existing `json` issue columns, so this is not a schema
migration. Exposing them through `/api/v1` is additive and optional.

**11. One merged, ordered `findings` list, computed once.**

**Tiebreak, explicitly**, because three severity values make ties the common case and an

## Success Criteria

Measured on **10 real pull requests** from repositories the author did not write, via the
harness in The Assignment: 10 PRs × 2 budgets × 2 repeats = **40 runs**.

- 100% of *displayed* line numbers point at a line that actually changed, **on the side it
  changed**. Findings with a nulled line count as correct-by-abstention, not failures.
- Median share of changed files sent to the model rises from the baseline the harness
  measures at 8,000 characters to the ceiling the step 3 arithmetic permits. The number is
  whatever the headroom supports; there is no target here independent of that measurement.
- Median findings surfaced per review is 3 or fewer, on the capped surfaces.
- Findings in the `console.log` / `any` / missing-`key` category: **≤1 across all 40 runs**.
  Not zero: the negative prompt instruction in step 10 reduces the model's unprompted
  disposition, it does not eliminate it, and no step filters that category post-hoc.
- The same PR reviewed twice yields the same verdict in at least 18 of 20 repeat pairs. A
  measured target with a stated tolerance, not a guarantee: the verdict derives from model
  output.
- One person who is not the author reads one review and can say what to fix without opening
  a second page.

## The Assignment

**Before building Phase 1b: measure premise 2, with a harness that can actually run.**

Do not attempt this through the product. Running PRism on someone else's repository needs a
webhook and admin rights, and nothing in the codebase enqueues a review for an arbitrary
public PR. Write a throwaway script: fetch a public PR's `.diff` by URL, call
`aiClient.callWithFallback()` directly with the existing prompt, print the result. An hour
of work, no webhook, no database.

**The harness must print, per run:** every issue; whether each claimed line is correct
against the real diff; files changed vs files sent; the Groq rate-limit headers; completion
latency; and once `verdict.ts` exists, the verdict. Those five outputs are what every
success criterion above is computed from.

**Run 10 merged PRs × 2 budgets (8,000 and 40,000) × 2 repeats.** The two budgets are the
part that matters. A single run at 8,000 measures line accuracy over a 200-line slice and
cannot tell you whether a bad number means the model invents lines (premise 2) or means the
model was shown 15% of the PR (premise 1). The entire phase ordering rests on separating
those. The two repeats give the verdict-stability number.

- High accuracy at both budgets: Phase 1b shrinks to almost nothing; go straight at Phase 2.
- Low at 8,000, high at 40,000: premise 1 was the real problem and step 3 is the whole fix.
- Low at both: premise 2 holds and the validator earns its place.

Second, smaller, same week: put PRism on one other person's repository and watch them read
one review without helping them. One person, one review. You are trying to fix a reading
experience and you have never watched anyone read it.
